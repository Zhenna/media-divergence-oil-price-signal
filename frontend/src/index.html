When does media divergence lead oil prices and when does it lag?
Six months of daily scoring across RT, Al Jazeera, CGTN, Reuters, BBC and NY Times reveals a regime-dependent relationship. During ambiguous geopolitical tension — before any kinetic event — narrative divergence between non-Western and Western outlets tends to precede oil price moves by 3 days. During clear kinetic events, the causal direction reverses: prices spike first on undisputed facts, and outlets diverge on interpretation afterward.

The boundary condition: the signal exists only when outlets have genuine interpretive freedom. Once a physical event is undeniable — a strait closes, a supreme leader is killed — all outlets report the same facts. Polarization collapses as a predictive signal; it becomes a lagging indicator of narrative competition instead.

▸ Supplementary — hypothesis design and statistical results
Three hypotheses were tested using Pearson correlation at lag 0–7 days between daily polarization signals and WTI closing prices. H1 and H2 returned null results. H3 — directional cluster divergence — returned a statistically significant signal (r=0.329, p=0.011). Expand to see the full r, p-value and lag for each hypothesis, plus a link to the source code.
